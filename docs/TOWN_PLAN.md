# Kazehana-chō town plan (Path to School)

The Path level must look like a real stretch of a small Japanese farming town, not a row of boxes.
This page records how rural Japanese houses and plots actually work. The plan data in
`tools/blender/town_plan.py` follows these rules, and an automatic review checks them before
anything is modelled.

## How rural Japanese houses and plots work

**Roads and access (Building Standards Act):**
- A building plot must touch a road at least 4 m wide for at least 2 m.
- Where an old road is narrower, the plot boundary is set back 2 m from the road's centre line.
- Rural towns therefore have:
  - one main road, about 6 m wide with white edge lines and no centre line
  - 4 m side lanes giving access to the plots behind
  - narrow concrete or gravel farm roads between fields

([Yokohama city guidance](https://translate-en.city.yokohama.lg.jp/business/bunyabetsu/kenchiku/annai/faq/douro.html), [setback explained](https://www.akiyajapan.com/glossary/setback-required))

**Orientation:**
- Main rooms face south for sunlight, so the house sits towards the north of its plot and the garden lies to the south.
- The entrance (genkan) faces whichever road gives access, so it is often on the north or a side.

([Housing in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_in_Japan), [traditional layout](https://architecturecourses.org/design/traditional-japanese-house-layout))

**House types in a farming town:**
- **Modern two-storey house** (1980s onwards), the most common:
  - gable roof (kirizuma), the most common form today, or hip roof (yosemune), the second most common
  - in kawara tiles or slate
  - ceramic siding walls, aluminium sash windows with shutter boxes (amado), and a south balcony for drying futons
  - a small porch roof over the genkan
- **Old farmhouse** (minka), on bigger plots:
  - an irimoya (hip-and-gable) kawara roof
  - dark board walls below and plaster above
  - an engawa veranda behind glass sliding doors on the south side
  - a separate shed or storehouse, stone walls and garden trees

([roof forms](https://blog.japanwondertravel.com/?p=22639), [irimoya](https://japanese-wiki-corpus.github.io/building/Irimoya-zukuri%20style.html), [minka](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minka))

**What makes a lived-in house:**
- At the gate: a mailbox and nameplate.
- Along the sides: air-conditioner outdoor units, a heat-pump water heater, gas cylinders or a meter box, rain gutters and downpipes.
- Outdoors: laundry poles on the balcony, a kei car or kei truck on the parking pad, potted plants.
- Overhead: a service wire from the nearest pole to each house.

**Plots:**
- Rural plots are about 200–450 m², with a 12–22 m frontage and 18–30 m depth.
- Parking for one or two cars is open to the road.
- The rest of the road frontage has a 1–1.2 m concrete block wall or a hedge, with a gate gap at the entrance path.

**Fields:**
- In early April, rice paddies are drained and ploughed.
- They are flooded and planted from late April into May, so the game shows ploughed fields in April and water later.

## The plan

The whole town is generated by `tools/blender/town_plan.py` (seeded, so it is always the same
town). Running `python tools/blender/town_plan.py` writes:
- `assets/town/town.json`: terrain, roads, rail, river, sites, houses, paddies, trees and poles
- `map.png`, `map_valley.png` and `map_centre.png`, which show the student homes when
  `households.json` exists

It is one connected place, about 3 × 2.4 km, with ~1500 people in about 500 households.

**Land:**
- A west–east valley floor of paddies, with forested hills to the north and south.
- Eleven side valleys (yato) cut into the hills, with flat floors and terraced paddies.
- The hill foot is irregular. Hamlets sit at the yato mouths, out of the flood plain.

**Roads:**
- The **old road** follows the north hill foot from hamlet to hamlet. The **Minami road** does the same on the south side.
- The **railway** and the **prefectural road** are later and straighter, across the floor.
- Lanes wind up into the hill foot the easy way and sometimes knot into loops.
- Consolidated paddies (75–125 m × 30 m) have a farm road on each column. Older, smaller paddies fill the edges.

**Town centre (around the station):**
- **Streets:** the station street / school road, the old road (the shotengai), a back street, an upper street, alleys, the station front street, the south-exit street and the Hikarigaoka danchi.
- **Buildings:** the post office, the town office branch, apartments, the temple and the Hachiman shrine.

**Hamlets:** Kami, Nishi, Yamanoue (up its yato), Higashi, Shimo, Minami-nishi, Minami,
Minami-higashi, Oku and Hazama.
- They are mostly farm and mixed houses.
- Farmhouses have sheds and windbreak trees (yashikirin) on their north and west sides.
- There are vegetable plots, bamboo behind the houses, and plots levelled behind retaining walls on the slopes.

**Trees:**
- On the hills: cedar and cypress plantations in rows, mixed broadleaf (konara, kunugi, shii, momiji) and mountain cherry.
- Around the houses: bamboo groves at the hamlets, and garden trees (persimmon, pine, maple, camellia, ume, maki).
- At shrines and temples: groves.
- Cherry trees along the levees by the school bridge and around the school.

**Home and school:**
- Tsukumodō (Uncle Kenji's shop and your home) is on the station street.
- The walk to school is 600 m (about 8 minutes). It goes past the station, over the level crossing, past the konbini and the Inari shrine, across the paddies and the river bridge, to the junior high in its bay under the south hills.
- That walk is the Path level.

**People:**
- The sim (`crates/sim/src/town.rs`) reads the houses and gives every one a household.
  - Families: grandparents, parents and children, with jobs that fit the district.
  - Surnames: each hamlet is dominated by a few old ones.
- All 99 students live with their families in those houses. Siblings are in different years.
- You and Uncle Kenji (Tsukumo) live at Tsukumodō.
- `cargo run -p sim --example households` writes who lives where.

## Automatic review

The script prints a review and exits non-zero on any problem. It checks:
- every plot has at least 2 m of frontage on a road at least 4 m wide, and its gate is on that road
- houses, parking and sheds stay inside their plot and do not overlap each other
- no plot overlaps another plot or a site
- plots on slopes need a retaining wall of no more than 6.5 m
- every house is within 40 m of a utility pole
- every lane and street joins the network
- the household total suits a town of about 1500 people (480–680)
- every hamlet has at least 15 households
- the walk to school is believable

The sim test `the_town_plan_houses_every_student` checks that every student has a house and parents on the real plan.
